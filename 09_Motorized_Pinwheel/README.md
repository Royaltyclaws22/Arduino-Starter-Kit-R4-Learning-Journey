# ⚙️ Motorized Pinwheel

In this project, I built an Arduino-controlled motorized pinwheel that demonstrates how a *pushbutton* can be used to control a *DC motor* through a simple digital circuit.

This project is based on Project 09 from the Arduino Starter Kit R4.

## 🛠️ Circuit Implementation

This project demonstrates a motorized pinwheel control system using an *Arduino UNO R4 WiFi Board*. It utilizes a *Pushbutton* as an input *switch* and controls a high-current *DC Motor* via a *MOSFET Transistor* driven by an external *9V Battery*, with a *Flyback Diode* for inductive kickback protection.

### 🔌 Power Connections
The circuit uses dual power sources with a common ground reference:
* **Arduino Power Rails:**
  * **5V** on Arduino $\rightarrow$ Positive ($+$) power rail of the *breadboard* (Red wire)
  * **GND** on Arduino $\rightarrow$ Negative ($-$) ground rail of the *breadboard* (Black wire)
* **External 9V Motor Power:**
  * A *9V battery snap connector* attaches directly to the *9V battery* terminals to supply dedicated power to the breadboard:
    * **Positive Lead (Red wire):** Connects to the right positive ($+$) power rail of the *breadboard*.
    * **Negative Lead (Black wire):** Connects to the right negative ($-$) ground rail of the *breadboard*.
* **Common Ground:**
  * A jumper wire connects the left negative ($-$) ground rail to the right negative ($-$) ground rail to establish a shared ground across the entire circuit.

### 🔘 Input Section: Pushbutton
A momentary *pushbutton* acts as the primary digital control input.

* **Power Connection:** One terminal of the *pushbutton* connects to the 5V positive ($+$) power rail.
* **Signal & Pull-Down Connection:**
  * The opposite terminal connects directly to Arduino digital pin `2`.
  * The same terminal connects to the common ground ($-$) rail through a *10kΩ pull-down resistor* to prevent floating states when the button is unpressed.

### ⚙️ Output Section: MOSFET & DC Motor
A *MOSFET Transistor* acts as a high-current *switch* to safely drive the *DC Motor* from the external *9V supply*.

* **MOSFET Pinout & Control:**
  * **Gate (Pin 1 / Left):** Connects to Arduino digital pin `9` to receive the *motor* activation signal.
  * **Drain (Pin 2 / Center):** Connects to the negative (black) wire of the *DC Motor*.
  * **Source (Pin 3 / Right):** Connects directly to the common ground ($-$) rail.
* **Motor Power & Flyback Protection:**
  * **Motor:** The positive (red) wire of the *DC Motor* connects to the external *9V* power rail.
  * **Flyback Diode:** Connected in parallel across the *motor* terminals (reverse-biased) to absorb inductive voltage spikes:
    * **Cathode (Striped side):** Connects to the *9V* *motor* power line.
    * **Anode:** Connects to the `Drain` of the *MOSFET* / negative side of the *motor*.

### ⚠️ Safety Tip
To ensure hardware safety, keep both the *Arduino board* disconnected from the computer (via the *USB-C cable*) and the *9V battery* detached while assembling the wiring. Ensure the *flyback diode* polarity is strictly verified (striped cathode towards *9V*) before powering the system to protect the *transistor* and *microcontroller* from voltage spikes generated by the spinning *motor* coils.

## ⚙️ How it Works

The circuit operates as an interactive motorized pinwheel system where the *Arduino UNO R4 WiFi board* monitors the state of a momentary *pushbutton* and actively controls a high-current *DC Motor* via a *MOSFET transistor* powered by an external *9V battery*:

* **Hardware Initialization (Setup Phase):**
  * Digital pin `9` is configured as an output to control the gate of the *MOSFET*.
  * Digital pin `2` is configured as an input to monitor the state of the *pushbutton*.

* **Digital Input Sensing (Inputs):**
  * The momentary *pushbutton* acts as the primary digital binary input connected to digital pin `2`.
  * Pressing the *switch* completes the connection to 5V, driving the input reading to `HIGH`.
  * Releasing the *switch* allows the *10kΩ pull-down resistor* to tie the pin safely to ground, driving the input reading to `LOW` and eliminating electrical noise.

* **Decision Logic (Program Logic):**
  * During operation, the *microcontroller* continuously checks the logical state of digital pin `2`.
  * The program evaluates the input condition:
    * If the *switch* state is `HIGH` (pressed), the *microcontroller* sets digital pin `9` to `HIGH` to engage the *motor*.
    * If the *switch* state is `LOW` (released), the *microcontroller* sets digital pin `9` to `LOW` to disengage the *motor*.

* **Transistor Switching & Motor Drive (Outputs):**
  * Driving digital pin `9` to `HIGH` applies voltage to the *MOSFET* `Gate`, allowing current to flow freely from `Drain` to `Source` and driving the *DC Motor* with the external *9V battery*.
  * Driving digital pin `9` to `LOW` closes the *transistor* `Gate`, immediately breaking the circuit path and stopping the *motor*.
  * The reverse-biased *flyback diode* across the *motor* terminals safely absorbs and dissipates inductive voltage spikes generated whenever power to the motor coils is interrupted.

* **Interactive User Behavior:**
  * **Mechanical Assembly & Preparation:** Create a paper pinwheel, attach it to the motor shaft using the included hub pieces and tape, connect a fully charged *9V battery*, and power the *Arduino board* over *USB*.
  * **Motorized Operation:** Pressing and holding the momentary *switch* on the *breadboard* causes the *DC motor* to spin the attached pinwheel very rapidly. Releasing the *switch* immediately cuts off power to the *motor*, bringing the pinwheel to a stop.

## 💻 Code

The program is organized into two main functions:

- `setup()` – Configures the *motor* pin as an output and the *pushbutton* pin as an input.
- `loop()` – Continuously reads the state of the *pushbutton* and turns the *motor* `ON` while the *switch* is pressed, otherwise keeping it turned `OFF`.

### Key Functions Used

- `pinMode()` – Configures the *motor* pin as an output and the *pushbutton* pin as an input.
- `digitalRead()` – Reads the current state of the *pushbutton*.
- `digitalWrite()` – Turns the *motor* `ON` or `OFF` depending on the state of the *pushbutton*.

## 🎓 What I Learned

Through building this project, I gained hands-on experience and practical knowledge about Arduino programming, power electronics, *motors*, and inductive loads:

- **MOSFET Transistors**
  - Learned that a *MOSFET transistor* allows controlling high-current and high-voltage power sources using the low-current digital output of an *Arduino board*.
  - Understood that a *MOSFET* acts as an electronic switch: when a voltage is applied to the `Gate`, it allows current to flow between the `Drain` and `Source` terminals, making it a voltage-controlled device.
  - Learned to identify a *MOSFET transistor* and correctly recognize its `Gate`, `Drain`, and `Source` pins (left, middle, and right respectively) to wire it properly in a circuit.

- **DC Motors and Electromagnetic Induction**
  - Understood that electromagnetic induction is the process by which a changing electrical current produces a changing magnetic field around a conductor.
  - Learned that a *DC motor* is an inductive device that uses a tightly wound copper coil to generate a magnetic field, causing the *motor* shaft to rotate.
  - Understood that the opposite process is also possible: when the shaft is rotated mechanically, the *motor* acts as a generator and produces electrical energy.
  - Learned to identify a *DC motor* and correctly recognize its power (red) and ground (black) wires for proper circuit connections.

- **Diodes and Back Voltage Protection**
  - Understood that when power to a *motor* is removed, the motor continues spinning briefly because of its inertia.
  - Learned that this spinning *motor* generates a reverse voltage (back EMF), which can damage electronic components such as the *MOSFET transistor*.
  - Understood the importance of connecting a *diode* in parallel with the *motor* to safely dissipate this reverse voltage and protect the rest of the circuit.
  - Learned that a *diode* is a polarized component that allows current to flow in only one direction.
  - Learned to identify a *diode* and correctly recognize its anode and cathode (marked with a stripe) in order to connect it properly.

- **Power Supply and Current Limitations**
  - Understood that Arduino digital pins can safely provide only up to `40 mA` of current, which is not enough to power a *motor* directly.
  - Learned why an external *9V battery* connected through a *battery snap connector* is required to provide the voltage and current needed by the *motor*.
  - Understood that the *MOSFET transistor* acts as a bridge between the Arduino's low-power control signal and the higher-power external *battery*, allowing the *board* to safely control the *motor* without supplying its operating current directly.
